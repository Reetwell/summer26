## 1. App name — recommendation

**The problem with "Build Your Body":** it's purely *descriptive*. That means (a) weak/near-impossible to trademark, (b) it collides with countless generic fitness listings so it'll never rank on its own words, and (c) it half-echoes the old body-image angle you deliberately left ("SummerBody"). Descriptive names lose in the App Store because the *name field* is your single biggest ranking lever — you want a distinctive brand there **plus** high-value keywords in the subtitle.

**Recommended strategy:** pick a short, ownable **brand word** for the name, and park the descriptive phrase ("build your body", "workout", "meal plan") in the **subtitle + keyword field** where it does SEO work. Best of both.

### Lead recommendation: **Forge**
Full listing name: **`Forge — Train · Eat · Focus`**

**Why:**
- Connotes *building/making yourself from raw effort* — keeps the "Build Your Body" spirit and maps perfectly onto the **effort-based rank** ethos, with **zero body-image baggage**.
- One syllable, brandable, works as a verb in copy ("forge your streak", "forge week 3"), strong @handle potential.
- Pairs cleanly with the **"Stay Focused"** tagline.

**Honest risk:** "Forge" is a real word in a crowded fitness class — there are existing "Forge"-named apps/brands. **Trademark clearance is not guaranteed; verify before committing.** If it's blocked, go to the backups.

### Ranked backups
1. **Groundwork** — `Groundwork — Train · Eat · Repeat`. "Put in the groundwork" = consistency ethos, non-body. More ownable than Forge; slightly longer.
2. **Rally** — `Rally — Your Training League`. Leans into the rank/**league** differentiator + community energy; motivational, non-shaming. Collision-prone (common word) — check hard.
3. **Tempo** — `Tempo — Train to Your Rhythm`. Calm, non-shaming, recovery-friendly (nods to readiness). Note: used by other fitness/finance products — check.
4. **Keep "Build Your Body" (BB)** — only as a fallback. If kept, treat "BB" as the short brand/handle, and rely entirely on subtitle/keywords for ranking. Accept that it won't be trademarkable or ownable.

## 3. Keyword field (Apple, 100 chars, comma-separated, no spaces, no plurals-of-singulars, don't repeat name/subtitle words)

```
workout,gym,fitness,mealplan,recipe,shopping,teen,student,beginner,training,diet,macro,streak
```
(93 chars)

**Rationale / notes:**
- Don't waste characters repeating words already in the name or subtitle — Apple indexes those.
- `teen`, `student`, `beginner` target the audience's self-search language (UK + US shared).
- **Deliberately excluded:** `weightloss`, `slim`, `shred`, `diet` pill-type terms — off-brand, teen-policy-risky, and they'd attract the wrong intent. (`diet` kept as it's neutral/high-volume; drop if it skews results.)
- **Spelling (UK+US):** these keywords are spelling-neutral. Where UK/US diverge in *screenshot copy* (e.g. "personalise"/"personalize"), use US spelling in the US listing and UK in the UK listing — Apple supports per-locale metadata (en-GB / en-US). Set both localisations.

## 6. Listing housekeeping (flagging, not owning)

- **Age rating:** set honestly for a 14–21 audience; health/fitness + the AADC context means the rating and the **privacy nutrition label** must be accurate before submission (product/legal lane — flagged in the project note).
- **"Kids category":** do **not** list in Apple's Kids category (that's under-13 and triggers stricter COPPA handling) — this is a teen/young-adult app.
- **Localisations to fill:** en-GB and en-US at minimum (spelling + "shopping list"/"grocery list" swap — US searches "grocery" more; consider `grocery` in the US keyword field).
